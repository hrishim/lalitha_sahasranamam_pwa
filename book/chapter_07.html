Nāma 601

दरान्दोलितदीर्घाक्षी

Samāsa-vigraha

दरम् (ईषद्यथा स्यात्तथा) आन्दोलितानि (चञ्चलानि) दीर्घाणि अक्षीणि यस्याः सा।

दरम् (भयम्) आन्दोलितं (नाशितं) यैः तादृशदीर्घाक्षीणि यस्याः सा।

Word by word

दरम् — slightly / fear.

आन्दोलितानि — moving, wavering.

चञ्चलानि — restless.

दीर्घाणि — long.

अक्षीणि — eyes.

यस्याः — whose.

सा — she.

भयम् — fear.

नाशितं — destroyed.

दरान्दोलितदीर्घाक्षी — the complete name, understood through the samasa above.

Meaning

(i) One whose long eyes, reaching up to the ears, slightly move.

(ii) Dara means a little; Her eyes gently move on all sides.

(iii) According to physiognomy, eyes extending to the ears are the best. Dara also means fear; Her eyes remove fear.

(iv) Her long wavering eyes allow Her compassionate glance to fall on all devotees.

Esoteric note

In the Saubhāgyabhāskara, Bhāskararāya unpacks this name across two distinct theological frameworks:

The Movement of Vimarśa: Under the first derivation, the slight, dynamic movement (दर-आन्दोलित) of Her long eyes represents the active pulse of विमर्श (Vimarśa — divine self-awareness) interacting with the static light of consciousness.

The Dissolution of Saṁsāra: Under the second track, दर means fear. Her glance acts as a powerful shield that completely eradicates the baseline existential fear of death, birth, and transience inside the heart of the practitioner.

दरहासोज्ज्वलन्मुखी

Samāsa-vigraha

Word by word

दरहासेन — by a gentle smile.

उज्ज्वलत् — shining.

मुखं — mouth/face.

यस्याः — whose.

सा — she.

दरहासोज्ज्वलन्मुखी — the complete name, understood through the samasa above.

Meaning

(i) One whose face shines with a smile.

(ii) Darahasa means a gentle smile. Her physical beauty and inner bliss are both indicated by this smile.

(iii) The dhyana verse calls Her Smitamukhi. This name may be compared with Darasmera Mukhambuja and the Trishati name Ishatsmitanana.

(iv) Dara also means fear; in war, Her terrible laughter destroys demons.

Esoteric note

Bhāskararāya details the intellectual transformations triggered by Her expression:

The Dawn of Vidyā: The gentle smile (darahāsa) is not a mundane emotional reaction. It represents the soft, luminous dawn of absolute knowledge (विद्या) breaking open. The radiance from Her smile enters the dark, dense blockages of the student's intellect, instantly pacifying spiritual doubt and infusing the consciousness with baseline joy.

Nāma 603

गुरुमूर्तिः

Samāsa-vigraha

गुरुरेव मूर्तिः शरीरं यस्याः सा।

Word by word

गुरुरेव — the Guru himself.

मूर्तिः — form, embodiment.

शरीरं — body.

यस्याः — whose.

सा — she.

गुरुमूर्तिः — the complete name, understood through the samasa above.

Meaning

(i) One who assumes the form of a teacher.

(ii) Mantra-shastras say that the teacher, the mantra, and the deity are one and should not be seen as different. The Sreechakra and the Self are also to be understood in the same non-different manner.

(iii) Gu means the darkness of ignorance and ru removes it; therefore Guru means one who removes ignorance. Sreedevee comes as the Guru and gives mantra-upadesha.

Esoteric note

Bhāskararāya defines the core channel of dynamic grace within the Śrīvidyā tradition:

The Human Gateway: The absolute supreme reality is too vast to be conceptualized directly by bound souls. Therefore, She assumes a tangible, human form as the श्रीगुरु (Śrīguru) to execute the transmission of initiatory power (śaktipāta). Bhāskararāya notes that the Guru is never to be viewed as an ordinary mortal; the preceptor is the visible, functional body of the Mother specialized for the liberation of the seeker.

Nāma 604

गुणनिधिः

Samāsa-vigraha

गुणानां निधिः।

गुणा व्यूहरूपा निधिसंख्या यस्याः सा।

गुणो रज्जुः सा चेह नौकाबन्धनी वटीरिकानाम्नी लक्षणया गृह्यते। सा नितरां धीयतेऽस्यामिति गुणनिधिः (मात्स्यकालीपुराणम्)।

Word by word

गुणानां — of qualities.

निधिः — treasure.

यस्याः — whose.

सा — she.

गुणनिधिः — the complete name, understood through the samasa above.

Meaning

(i) One who is the treasure-house of qualities.

(ii) Sankhya doctrine speaks of three qualities, sattva, rajas, and tamas, with countless modifications. She is the repository of all such qualities.

(iii) Guna also means aggregate or vyuha; nine such vyuhas are described. Since She is the form of these, She is called Gunanidhi.

(iv) Guna also means rope; Puranic accounts of the rope used at pralaya are connected with Her stabilising power.

Esoteric note

Bhāskararāya explores this across standard cosmic management and Purāṇic alchemical metaphors:

The Reservoir of Cosmic Virtues: She is the infinite repository (nidhi) of all divine splendors, cosmic qualities, and manifestations.

The Anchor of the Soul: Under the Mātsyakālī Purāṇa track, guṇa denotes the heavy rope used to tie a vessel. She is the secure treasure dock that holds the binding rope of the individual soul's boat, keeping it firmly anchored and safe from capsizing in the turbulent storm waves of material illusion.

गुहजन्मभूः — the complete name, understood through the samasa above.

Meaning

Esoteric note

Bhāskararāya traces this from mythological history to deep internal coordinates:

The Parent of the Dynamic Power: Mythologically, She is the mother of Skanda (Guha), the lord of intense spiritual action.

The Source of the Cave Dwellers: Esoterically, गुहा (guhā) refers to the hidden, secret cavern of the heart where the individual living souls (जीवाः) reside. She is the primordial baseline space out of which all individual sparks of life are born and projected.

The Sacred Internal Sky: दहराकाश (Daharākāśa) is the tiny, luminous ether chamber hidden deep inside the cavity of the human heart. The Upaniṣad reveals that this internal space contains the entire macrocosm. She is that exact pristine, unblemished inner sky where the absolute can be directly realized when the outward senses are quieted.

Word by word

प्रतिपत्तिथिः — the first lunar day.

मुख्या — principal, beginning.

यस्मिन् — in which.

राकातिथिः — the full-moon day.

(i) One who is worshipped in the circle of lunar days beginning with Pratipat and ending with the full moon.

(ii) Pratipat is the first lunar day and Raka is the full-moon day. She is worshipped through all fifteen days according to tantric rites.

Esoteric note

Bhāskararāya details the integration of time and mantric geometry within this name:

The Mistress of Lunar Time: In Śrīvidyā cosmology, She is non-different from Time itself. The fifteen lunar days are governed by fifteen specific crescent energy deities known as the तिथि-नित्याः (Tithi-Nityāḥ), running from Pratipat to Pūrṇimā. She stands as the sixteenth absolute background light (Ṣoḍaśī) that animates this complete cycle, completely surrounding and permeating the calendar array.

Meaning

Esoteric note

In the Saubhāgyabhāskara, Bhāskararāya unpacks the term कला (Kalā) across multiple systematic tracks:

The Manifested Fractions: Kalā represents the digits of the moon, the sixty-four cultural arts, or the functional fractions into which the absolute energy divides itself to construct the material universe. She is the soul of all these diverse expressions, animating the components while remaining internally undivided.

Samāsa-vigraha

सर्वेषां चतुर्णामर्थानां पुरुषार्थानां दात्री।

Word by word

Word meanings are read directly from the samāsa expression above.

Meaning

(i) One who gives all aims of life.

(ii) She grants the four puruṣārthas: dharma, artha, kāma and mokṣa.

(iii) Thus every true human aim is obtained through Her grace.

Esoteric note

Bhāskararāya outlines Her complete, balanced fulfillment of human aspiration:

The Dispenser of the Four Aims: She handles the complete grid of the four human objectives: धर्म (righteousness), अर्थ (wealth), काम (desire), and मोक्ष (liberation). She ensures that Her devotees do not suffer material lack while seeking spirit, nor get trapped in wealth without spirit, organizing a balanced distribution of all human aims.

Nāma 699

सावित्री

Samāsa-vigraha

सवितुर्जगत्प्रसूतेः परशिवस्येयं सावित्री।

Word by word

Word meanings are read directly from the samāsa expression above.

Meaning

(i) She is Sāvitrī.

(ii) Savitṛ is the creator or impeller of the world; She is the Śakti of that supreme source.

(iii) The name also recalls the Gāyatrī-Sāvitrī contemplation connected with the Sun and the supreme being.

Esoteric note

Bhāskararāya details Her identity with the absolute source of light:

The Power of the Divine Creator: सवीतृ (Savitā) refers to the supreme generator of the universe—Paramaśiva. She is His non-dual dynamic power (Sāvitrī). This confirms Her absolute identity with the inner essence of the Gāyatrī Mantra, acting as the brilliant light that awakens the spiritual intellect.

Nāma 700

सच्चिदानन्दरूपिणी

Samāsa-vigraha

सत्त्वं चित्त्वमानन्दश्च रूपं स्वरूपमस्या इति।

Word by word

Word meanings are read directly from the samāsa expression above.

Meaning

(i) One whose form is existence, consciousness and bliss.

(ii) Sat, cit and ānanda together constitute Brahman.

(iii) That Brahman is Śrīdevī; this completes the group of kalās described here.

Esoteric note

In the Saubhāgyabhāskara, Bhāskararāya concludes this major sequence by stating Her absolute philosophical definition:

The Supreme Definition of Brahman: This name frames Her identity with the unconditioned absolute. She is not a passing manifestation; Her entire essential body is made of nothing but pure, uncorrupted, eternal existence (Sat), infinite, self-luminous awareness (Cit), and boundless, unshakeable bliss (Ānanda), serving as the ultimate refuge of reality.
